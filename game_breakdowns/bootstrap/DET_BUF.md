## 5. THREAT INTELLIGENCE

**How a Threat designation works.** A Threat fires when a player's own season rank in a specific statistical category and the upcoming opponent's defensive rank in that exact same category both clear a tier's specific threshold at the same time — it is the convergence of genuinely elite individual production and a genuinely exploitable matchup in the same stat, not either signal by itself, that the deterministic Threat Engine is built to catch. The three tiers use these exact thresholds (player rank is 1st-best; defense rank is 1st-toughest, so a high defense-rank number means a weak defense):

- **Nuclear** — player ranks top 3 in the category AND the opponent's defense ranks 30th or worse in that same category
- **Elite** — player ranks top 5 AND opponent defense ranks 28th or worse
- **Standard** — player ranks top 10 AND opponent defense ranks 23rd or worse

Double/Triple/Quadruple reflects how many categories converged for that player: Double is one category meeting both thresholds; Triple adds a second category where the player's own rank alone clears the threshold even if the defense side doesn't; Quadruple means two or more categories fully converged. **No Nuclear-tier designation fired for any player in this matchup.**

That clears Standard's 23rd-or-worse threshold but falls short of Elite's 28th-or-worse bar, which is why the designation caps at Standard rather than climbing higher: St. Brown's own production is elite, but Buffalo's defense, while below average, isn't among the very weakest tier in this specific category. St. Brown's own yardage rank (21st) is a real counterweight worth naming honestly — his volume is dominant, his per-catch explosiveness (6.7 yards/reception in Week 1) is not, which is consistent with a possession-receiver role rather than a big-play one this week.
