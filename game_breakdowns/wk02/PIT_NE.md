## 5. THREAT INTELLIGENCE

**How the Threat system works.** A Threat designation fires only when two things converge in the same stat category: a player's own SEASON rank in that category, and the upcoming opponent's defensive rank in that same category, both clearing a tier's specific thresholds. Rank is 1st-best for the player side; for the defense side, a HIGH rank number means a WEAK defense. The three tiers, exactly as defined: **Nuclear** requires the player ranked top 3 AND the opponent's defense ranked 30th or worse in that category; **Elite** requires top 5 AND defense ranked 28th or worse; **Standard** requires top 10 AND defense ranked 23rd or worse. "Double," "Triple," and "Quadruple" describe how many categories converged — Double is one full convergence; Triple adds a second category where the player's own rank alone clears that tier's threshold even if the defense side falls short; Quadruple means two or more categories fully converge on both sides.

**Drake Maye (NE) — Elite tier, Triple type, AND a separate Standard tier, Double type.** Two categories are involved. In pass yards, Maye's own 2025 season rank was 4th (258.5 ypg) — clearing Elite's top-5 threshold — against a Pittsburgh defense ranked 30th in pass yards allowed (261.0 ypg) — clearing Elite's defense-side threshold of 28th-or-worse by two full spots. That single category fully converges at the Elite tier. In pass touchdowns, Maye's own rank was 5th (1.82 TDs/game, 31 on the season) — also clearing Elite's top-5 player threshold — but Pittsburgh's defense ranked only 25th in touchdowns allowed (30 on the season), which falls short of Elite's 28th-or-worse defense threshold. Because Maye's own rank alone clears the Elite bar in this second category even without the defense side converging, it qualifies as the "extra" category that makes the pass-yards designation a Triple type rather than a plain Double. That same touchdown category, evaluated independently against the lower Standard bar (top 10 player, defense 23rd-or-worse), fully converges on both sides (player 5th, defense 25th) — which is why it also generates its own separate Standard-tier, Double-type designation.

What this means in plain terms: Maye's 2025 season-long passing profile is genuinely elite by volume, and Pittsburgh's pass defense is genuinely one of the league's weakest by the same measures — not a marginal edge, but a real, multi-category statistical convergence. It does not, by itself, account for Maye's rough Week 1 turnover profile (3 interceptions) or the receiver-usage uncertainty around A.J. Brown (Section 6) — a Threat reflects season-long statistical stability on both sides, not a guarantee against those complicating factors, and both are worth weighing against this designation rather than around it.

- **Pat Freiermuth (PIT) and Hunter Henry (NE)** both play favorable matchups against soft tight end defenses (Section 4), but neither player's own season rank in receiving yards clears even the Standard tier's top-10 threshold — Henry ranked 13th, Freiermuth 24th — so no designation fired for either despite the real matchup edge underneath.

- **Mack Hollins and Romeo Doubs** both face a genuinely weak Pittsburgh WR defense (28th in yards allowed), but neither player's own season rank (61st and 47th, respectively) comes remotely close to qualifying — the matchup edge exists, but not at a level either individual's volume supports.
