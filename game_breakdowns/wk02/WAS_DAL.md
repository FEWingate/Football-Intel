## 5. THREAT INTELLIGENCE

**How a Threat designation works:** Football Intel's deterministic Threat Engine fires only when two things converge in the *same specific statistical category* — a player's own SEASON rank in that category, and the upcoming opponent's defensive rank in that same category — both clearing a tier's exact threshold. It is not enough for a player to simply be good, or an opponent to simply be bad; the same category has to be weak on both sides at once. The three tiers use these exact thresholds (player rank is 1st-best; defense rank is 1st-toughest, so a high defense-rank number means a weak defense):

- **Nuclear** — player ranks top 3 in the category AND the opponent's defense ranks 30th or worse in that same category
- **Elite** — player ranks top 5 AND opponent defense ranks 28th or worse
- **Standard** — player ranks top 10 AND opponent defense ranks 23rd or worse

"Double," "Triple," and "Quadruple" describe how many categories converged for a given player — Double is one fully-converged category; Quadruple means two or more categories both cleared their thresholds independently.

**A data note before applying it here:** Washington's side of this evaluation lists "Marcus Mariota" and "Treylon Burks" among its evaluated starters, both of whom are `roster_only` in this evidence package (zero 2026 games logged) — meaning the Threat Engine appears to have evaluated Washington's 2025 depth-chart snap leaders rather than the confirmed 2026 Week 1 starters (Jayden Daniels played 100% of snaps at QB; Stefon Diggs, Terry McLaurin, and Antonio Williams were Washington's actual Week 1 receivers). This is worth flagging plainly rather than silently working around. Practically, it does not change the outcome below — none of Washington's evaluated starters (Mariota, Jacory Croskey-Merritt, Terry McLaurin, Treylon Burks, Ben Sinnott) drew a Threat designation regardless.

**Washington: no Threat designations fired for any evaluated player.** This should be read plainly — it does not mean nothing interesting is happening for Washington's offense (Diggs' target-share jump above is real, evidence-backed content), only that no player's season rank and Dallas's matching defensive rank in the same category cleared a tier threshold together.

**Dallas: three Threat designations fired.**

- **Dak Prescott — Elite (Double), pass_yds.** Prescott's 2025 season pass-yards rate ranked 3rd in the league; Washington's defense allowed the 28th-most passing yards per game in 2025. Both clear the Elite threshold (top 5 and defense rank 28+) in the same category. He separately clears the Standard threshold in pass_tds (ranked 6th, Washington ranked 30th allowing touchdowns), which is why the engine lists a second, Standard-tier Double for that category as well. Connecting this to the rest of the report: Prescott's Week 1 line (175 yards) was modest relative to his season rate, but came against a tougher-graded opponent (Giants) than Washington projects to be — this Threat reflects season-long stability, not a guarantee, but nothing in Washington's specific matchup profile argues against it.
- **Javonte Williams — Standard (Double), rush_yds.** His 2025 rush-yards rate ranked 7th; Washington's run defense ranked 30th allowing rushing yards to backs specifically. Both clear the Standard threshold (top 10 and defense rank 23+). His actual Week 1 role (66.7% rush share) is, if anything, a step up from the 2025 season rate this Threat is built on — a point of alignment rather than tension.
- **George Pickens and CeeDee Lamb — both Standard (Quadruple), rec_yds and rec.** Both receivers' 2025 rec-yards ranks (4th and 5th, respectively) and reception-count ranks (10th and 7th) each independently clear the Standard threshold against Washington's 27th-ranked pass defense in each category — two categories converging for each player is what produces the "Quadruple" label.
